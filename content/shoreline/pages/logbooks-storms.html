What the keepers wrote during the great storms

In the logbooks, the worst nights of the century are told in a few calm lines.

By Ruth Calder · November 23, 2026

The entry for November 9, 1913 is four lines long. The wind was from the northeast, the glass was falling fast and the keeper had climbed to the lamp room every hour through the night to clear ice from the windows.

Three ships were lost within sight of the light that week. The keeper recorded each one by name and the time it was last seen, then went back to the weather.

Reading the volumes in order, a pattern appears: the bigger the storm, the shorter the entry. The keepers were too busy to write.
