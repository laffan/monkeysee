The humpback whale

Long-finned, acrobatic and famously musical, the humpback is the whale most people picture when they think of whales.

Humpbacks live in every ocean, from the polar seas where they feed to the warm, shallow waters where they give birth. Their flippers can reach a third of their body length, the longest of any whale. Each animal carries a unique pattern of black and white on the underside of its tail, which researchers use to recognise individuals for decades.

Feeding together

In summer a humpback may eat more than a tonne of krill and small fish a day. Groups hunt with a trick no other whale uses: they swim in a rising spiral and blow a curtain of bubbles that traps a school of fish. When the ring closes, the whales surge up through the middle with their mouths open.

Song

Male humpbacks sing long, structured songs that can last for twenty minutes and be repeated for hours. Every male in a population sings nearly the same song, and the song slowly changes over a season. Read more about why humpbacks sing.

Migration

Most humpbacks spend the summer feeding near the poles and swim to the tropics to breed in winter, eating almost nothing on the way. Follow the longest commute in the animal kingdom.
