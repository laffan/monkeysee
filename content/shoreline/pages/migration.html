Migration

The longest commute

Every year humpbacks swim from cold feeding grounds to warm calving lagoons and back, a trip of up to 25,000 kilometres.

The journey is driven by two needs that are thousands of kilometres apart. Food is in the cold, rich polar seas, while calves are safest in warm, calm water with few predators. Mothers fast for months and live off their blubber while they nurse.

Tagged whales hold a remarkably straight course over open ocean, correcting for currents as they go. How they navigate is still an open question: the sun, the earth's magnetic field and the shape of the sea floor may all play a part.
