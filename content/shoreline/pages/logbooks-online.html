A century of lighthouse logbooks goes online

Volunteers have scanned every page kept at Gull Point Light since 1891, from weather reports to the price of lamp oil.

By Ruth Calder · November 22, 2026

For seventy-three years, someone at Gull Point Light wrote down the wind, the weather and every ship that passed. The logbooks, forty-one leather volumes, sat in a museum basement until a group of volunteers began scanning them two winters ago.

This week the archive opened to the public. Anyone can now page through the keepers' handwriting, search it by date and read about storms, shipwrecks and the slow work of keeping a lamp burning every night.

"You start reading for the weather and you end up reading about the people," said Ana Ferreira, who led the project. The keepers noted births, illnesses and visitors in the same careful hand they used for the barometer.

Historians say the records are also a rare long run of daily weather on this coast. Climate researchers at the state university have already begun turning the entries into data.
