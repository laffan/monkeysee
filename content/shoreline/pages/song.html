Behaviour · Sound

Why humpbacks sing

A humpback's song is one of the most complex displays in the animal world, and we still don't fully know what it is for.

Only males sing, mostly on the breeding grounds and mostly at night. A song is built like a piece of music: short units of sound form phrases, phrases repeat into themes, and a full song runs through several themes in a fixed order.

The strangest thing about the song is how it spreads. When a new song appears in one ocean basin, it can sweep from population to population across thousands of kilometres within two years, until every male is singing the new version.

What the song might mean

Most researchers think the song helps males attract females or keep rivals at a distance, but singers are rarely approached by females at all. Others suggest it works like a sonar ping, or a way for males to rank themselves without fighting.
